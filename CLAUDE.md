## What this is

**Bare Bones Custom Night** (it was called *Night Shift* until 2026-10-31; the repo name `bbcn` is its initials) is a browser game in the style of Five Nights at Freddy's (FNAF), drawn in ASCII art and played with the mouse and keyboard. You sit in a security office from 12 AM to 6 AM, close two doors, flash two door lights, watch seven cameras on a monitor that also has a vent map (and a task list), wind a music box, wear a mask, play audio lures in the vents, close pop-up ads and ration your power. Nights come in three modes: Standard, Generator and Tasks.

- **The game is split into files by job:** `index.html` (structure), `style.css` (looks) and eleven scripts in `js/`. The game itself has no build step, no dependencies and no sound files; only the public website gets a small build (see "Hosting, deploy and analytics"). Jake is learning from this code, so every file has a header comment and learning-oriented comments.

### The threats (custom night: each has its own level, 0–20)

There is **no 5-night story**. Each game is a single custom night, and the setup screen sets a level for each threat. A creature at level *L* has an L/20 chance to move on each of its movement ticks. **Level 0 switches a threat off** (setup shows `OFF`): shapes never move, Lenny's box never unwinds and (: never wakes. The one exception is a power-out, which always sends ◆ or ▲, preferring one that's switched on.

**★ Modifiers:** each threat also has a checkbox on the setup screen (Space toggles the selected row's) that adds a twist, separate from its level. Each ticked modifier adds `MOD_BONUS` (+5) to the score, so the maximum is 325 (13 threats × 25; it was 225 before Ξ § ¥ ©); a modifier on a level-0 threat does nothing, earns nothing, and its checkbox is greyed out. Every modifier was designed to be fair, meaning it has a tell or a counter:

## Design decisions (and why)

- **Door lights are back, but only for ■.** Jake originally removed them because lights implied a shape waits at the door, slowing the pace. They returned with the ■ Square on request, as its only counter. ◆ ▲ ● still work without lights.
- **Shapes slowed when ■ and (: arrived.** The movement intervals of ◆ ▲ ● went up 1.5x (4.8/5.2/6.4 to 7.2/7.8/9.6, before `PACE`) to make room for two more threats.
- **Every threat got about 25% easier** when modifiers were added: ◆ ▲ ● and ■ intervals ×1.25 (9.0 / 9.75 / 12.0 / 15.0 before `PACE`; ■ later went to 18.0), Lenny's `boxDrain` ×0.75, and Smiley's `smileyTime` ×1.25 (still level-based). Smiley's wake chance was deliberately left unchanged, on request.
- **Balance was checked with a bot.** On 2026-10-31 a bot played Nightmare 60 times per setup, reacting only to in-game cues after a human-like delay and doing one action at a time. It beat Nightmare 98–100% with about 1 s reactions, always with 60%+ power left. Four fixes followed: shapes linger at shut doors (power now matters; **later removed on request**, so doors are cheap again and power matters less than the numbers below say); ■'s modifier stopped offering a door trick (it had made ■ easier; it's now Flinch); (: also wakes on a timer (it could be avoided entirely); and Trickster fakes only while the camera is up (fakes were harmless otherwise). Patient still barely mattered after those fixes, so it now also waits when you shut it out mid-attack (once per visit). After the fixes, a smart bot (one that winds Lenny early and pushes ■ back only partway when busy) wins Nightmare about 98% of the time with 1 s reactions, ending with about 37% power. A careless bot wins about 10%.
- **Level 20 was eased so Total Nightmare can be beaten** (on request: "maybe like 10% with these bots"; Total Nightmare itself must stay at the maximum). Removing any one modifier still left the bot at 0 wins, so the fix is game-wide: the reaction window at level 20 went from 2.4 to 2.9 s, Lenny's box at level 20 drains half as fast, the light push at level 20 is 0.8 s (was 1.0), `MASK_HOLD` is 2.0 s (was 2.5), the Visitor waits 12–22 s at level 20 (was 8–14), ■ moves every 9 s (was 7.5), Ж moves half as often and gets `crawlerWindow` (reaction window + 2.5 s) in your office. Low levels barely changed. The bot now wins Total Nightmare 52 of 500 times (it was 0; 69 after the Crawler's longer route); every other challenge got easier for it too, and the `bot` numbers were all re-measured.
- **Modifiers must be fair and interesting.** The first proposal (skipping rooms, a silent ■, an orb invisible on cameras, and so on) was rejected as unfair or dull. Every modifier now has a tell or a counter, and its numbers don't depend on the threat's level.
- **The vents / ¥ / © / modes drop (2026-11-01, on request).** Asked for: a Generator mode (start at 0, hold to charge, can't do anything else), a Tasks mode (finish tasks on the computer, paused if you leave it), two vent threats with free audio lures, a second mask threat, an ad threat, lots of new challenges, revamped old ones, and 13/20 plus a new max in all three modes, without changing any old challenge's mode. Choices made with the owner: the computer is the existing monitor with a tab per system; tasks are hold-to-progress, not mini-games; holding the generator opens everything; vents use a map plus lures. ¥ was changed during planning from "only while the monitor is up" to "any time, but quicker to send away". Old challenges revamped with the new threats (each got a higher `v`): Masquerade (+ ¥ 10), Crawl space (+ Ξ 8), Channel surfing (+ © 10), Mixed bag and Graveyard shift (all 13 at 10 and 12).
- **New threats go at the end of `SETUP`**, so levels saved by older versions keep lining up: `[◆, ▲, ●, Lenny, ■, (:]`.
- **The threats are Unicode symbols, not characters.** That was Jake's direction. Lenny and (: are the only faces. The three newest (Ж ¤ Ψ) were asked to be "random Unicode symbols" that are **not shapes**, and then not to be "just colored balls of the character": they're small ASCII **drawings** (`SPRITES` in `art.js`, written with `String.raw` so backslashes stay literal) with their symbol as the eyes or body, in a camera/doorway size, an office size and a jumpscare size (shown at 2.2× via `.scare-big`). Spaces inside a drawing hide what's behind it; spaces around it don't.
- **W does two jobs.** On the cameras, hold it to wind the music box; in the office, press it to put the mask on or off. That works because the mask can't be used on the cameras (on request). The mask blocks only the cameras (on request: "you can use everything but the cams while wearing it").
- **Custom night instead of 5 nights.** Jake wanted every threat to have its own adjustable level.
- **Doors work while the camera is up.** Jake asked for this so you keep watching while you close a door.
- **The orb is invisible at the door.** Jake wanted it identified by its louder audio cue only.
- **The orb stalls when both doors are shut.** Requested so that shutting both doors doesn't simply send it away: it waits (costing you power for both doors) and then attacks whichever door opens first. It's the `stalls` flag in `data.js` and the `m.stalled` checks in `updateMob` (`game.js`). The stall only starts when it *arrives* at a doorway with both doors shut; a door shut during the normal reaction window still sends it back.
- **Power drains only while in use.** Jake removed the idle drain and asked for faster per-use drain to compensate. It went from 0.10% idle + 0.12% per item to 0% idle + 0.20% per item.
- **Short nights, heavy drain.** The hour was cut from 60 to 30 seconds (3-minute nights), and the drain per item was doubled to 0.40% at the same time. Doubling it keeps the same share of the battery per in-game hour, so power stays just as tight over the shorter night.
- **Everyone speeds up with the clock.** `PACE` in `config.js` is `60 / HOUR_SECONDS` (2 with 30-second hours). The shapes' movement intervals are divided by it and Lenny's box drain is multiplied by it, so a short night holds as many visits and windings as the original 6-minute one did. The door reaction window is left alone on purpose, because it's about human reaction time, not the length of the night.
- **No strobe on the jumpscare.** It used to flash the whole screen red; that was removed on request. The jumpscare only shakes now. Don't add flashing back.
- **The rename kept the internal names.** The `window.nightShift` test hook and the `nightshift:` prefix on saved `localStorage` keys stay as they are, so saved levels and best scores aren't lost.
- **Visual style:** a single dark amber CRT terminal. The fonts are VT323 for display text and IBM Plex Mono for the art, both loaded from Google Fonts. The look is dark on purpose, so there's no light theme.

### Gotchas

- **`index.html` is a page fragment.** It has no `<!doctype>`, `<html>`, `<head>` or `<body>`, because the Artifact publisher adds them. Don't add a skeleton.
- **The game must also work when `index.html` is double-clicked.** Jake plays it that way. Two things make that work, so keep both:
  - `<meta charset="utf-8">` (plus a viewport meta) at the very top of `index.html`. Without it, symbols like ◆ turn into gibberish.
  - `[hidden] { display: none !important; }` in `style.css`. The artifact wrapper supplies this rule, but a local file doesn't. Without it, every hidden overlay with a `display` rule shows at once; for example, the Game Over screen covers the title.
- **Avoid global names that clash with browser built-ins.** Because the scripts share the global scope, a name like `screen` could collide with `window.screen`. That's why the variable is called `currentScreen`.
- **Keep art ASCII-only**, except the threat symbols. Non-ASCII glyphs come from fallback fonts with different widths and break the alignment. Symbols get the `.sym` class (inline-block, `1ch` wide) to keep their column.
- **Lenny's face needs a system font.** IBM Plex Mono can't draw its combining marks (U+0361 and U+035C), which show up as a box. `.face` and `.bigface` use a `system-ui` font stack. Inside the ASCII grid the face is one cell followed by 7 empty cells, so it spans 8 columns.
- **To add a camera:** update `ROOMS` and `CAM_ORDER` (`data.js`), `ROOM_ART` and `SLOTS` (`art.js`), the `#map` buttons (`index.html`), `.map`'s `grid-template-areas` (`style.css`), the `1–7` key check (`input.js`) and the help text.
- **To add a leveled threat:** add it to `CREATURES` in `data.js`, or handle it separately like `LENNY`, and make sure it lands in `SETUP`. Old saved levels are padded automatically.
- **To add a new script file:** add a `<script src>` line in `index.html` in the right load-order position, and add it to the `files` map when publishing. `tools/build.js` and the syntax check read the script list from `index.html`, so they need no change.
- **The `window.nightShift` test hook:** `nightShift.state` shows the current night, and `nightShift.speed = 20` fast-forwards time. It's harmless to leave in.
